# PlateCheck rule catalog (rules v0.4.0)

Scientific review status: Not yet reviewed by a qPCR domain expert.

Generated by `node cli.mjs --rules-md`. Severity belongs to the rule, not a generic score.

| Rule | Group | Category | Severity | What is checked | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `malformed_row` | Structure | Data issue | high | Each data row has the same number of fields as the header. | A row with missing or extra fields may be shifted. It was excluded from every other check rather than guessed. |
| `invalid_well_coordinate` | Structure | Data issue | high | Well labels are valid plate positions (A1 to P24). | An invalid position usually means a column shift or typo, so the row cannot be placed on the plate. |
| `duplicate_well_assignment` | Structure | Data issue | high | Each well holds at most one result per target. | Two results for one well and target can mean a merged export or mapping error, and averages could double-count. |
| `unmapped_role` | Structure | Missing context | medium | Roles or tasks in the file map to a known role. | Labels such as “Neg Ctrl” are ambiguous (NTC, no-RT or a negative sample). The well is treated as a sample until you assign a role. |
| `instrument_omit` | Structure | Review warning | low | Wells excluded in the instrument software are surfaced rather than silently dropped. | An omitted well changes the instrument's means and standard curves; the reason should be recorded. |
| `unmapped_well` | Structure | Missing context | medium | Every used well has a target, and sample-type wells have a sample label. | Unlabelled wells cannot be grouped into replicates or matched to controls. |
| `non_numeric_ct` | Structure | Data issue | medium | Ct/Cq values are numbers or a recognised no-amplification marker (for example Undetermined, NaN). | An unrecognised value cannot be used in calculations and may indicate an export problem. |
| `missing_ct` | Structure | Review warning | low | Sample and standard wells report a Ct/Cq. | No amplification in a sample well may be real (target absent or below detection) or a failed reaction. |
| `empty_well_signal` | Structure | Review warning | medium | Wells marked empty do not report a Ct/Cq. | A Ct in an unused well suggests the plate map does not match the plate. |
| `map_wells_not_in_data` | Structure | Data issue | medium | Every well in the imported plate-map template appears in the results file. | Mapped wells with no data can mean the wrong file, plate orientation or a dropped well. |
| `data_wells_not_in_map` | Structure | Missing context | medium | Every well in the results file appears in the imported plate-map template. | Wells outside the plate map keep their file labels only, which may not be what was intended. |
| `bio_unit_in_multiple_groups` | Structure | Data issue | medium | Each biological unit belongs to a single experimental group. | A unit labelled in two groups usually indicates a labelling error that would mix conditions. |
| `replicate_structure_inferred` | Replicates | Missing context | low | Biological units (independent samples) are labelled separately from technical replicate wells. | PlateCheck assumed each sample name is one biological unit. Technical wells are never counted as independent biological observations. |
| `insufficient_replicates` | Replicates | Not assessed | low | Each technical replicate group has at least two numeric Ct/Cq values. | With fewer than two values, dispersion cannot be estimated. |
| `replicate_dispersion` | Replicates | Review warning | medium | Sample standard deviation of Ct/Cq within a technical replicate group is at or below the threshold. With the Ct-dependent option on, the threshold for a group is the larger of the set value and a multiple of the SD expected from Poisson sampling at that group's mean Ct. | Wide technical spread can come from pipetting, low template or an outlier well. No value was removed or down-weighted. |
| `late_ct` | Replicates | Review warning | low | Sample replicate groups have a mean Ct/Cq at or below the late-Ct setting. | Past about Ct 35 a well holds roughly 10 template copies or fewer, so how many copies land in each well is down to chance. Replicates scatter, some wells stay empty, and the result is closer to detected / not detected than to a quantity. This is a note, not a fault. |
| `missing_control` | Controls | Missing context | medium | Each target has the controls the selected rule set expects. | Without the control, contamination or reaction failure for this target cannot be assessed from the data. |
| `control_not_applicable` | Controls | Informational | info | Expected controls that a user marked not applicable are recorded with the reason. | Keeps the decision visible in the report. |
| `no_control_roles` | Controls | Missing context | medium | At least one well has a control or standard role. | Without identified controls, none of the control checks can run. |
| `ntc_amplification` | Controls | Review warning | high below the NTC threshold, otherwise medium | No-template controls do not amplify. Amplification earlier than the NTC Ct threshold is rated high, later amplification medium. | Amplification in an NTC can indicate contamination or primer-dimer. The cause cannot be determined from Ct alone. |
| `positive_control_failed` | Controls | Data issue | high | Positive controls report a Ct/Cq. | If the positive control failed, negative results for this target cannot be told apart from reaction failure. |
| `nrt_amplification` | Controls | Review warning | medium | No-RT controls do not amplify. | Signal without reverse transcription can indicate genomic DNA. Its distance from the sample Ct matters for interpretation. |
| `nrt_margin` | Controls | Review warning | medium | An amplifying no-RT control comes up at least the configured number of cycles after the mean Ct of the samples it controls (the sample it was made from when the names match, otherwise all samples of that target). | The gap says how much of the sample signal could be genomic DNA: 5 cycles is about 3%, 3.3 cycles about 10%, 1 cycle about half. A small gap means the sample Ct is not a clean measure of the RNA. |
| `std_curve_missing_quantity` | Standard curve | Data issue | medium | Standard wells have a positive numeric quantity. | Standards without a quantity cannot be placed on the curve. |
| `std_curve_insufficient` | Standard curve | Not assessed | low | A standard curve has at least the configured number of dilution levels with numeric Ct/Cq. | Efficiency and linearity are not meaningful with too few levels. |
| `std_curve_summary` | Standard curve | Informational | info | Linear fit of Ct/Cq against log10 quantity. | Records slope, R² and efficiency for the report. |
| `std_curve_efficiency` | Standard curve | Review warning | medium | Efficiency computed as 10^(−1/slope) − 1 is within the configured range. | Efficiency far from 100% can bias relative quantification methods that assume equal efficiency. |
| `std_curve_r2` | Standard curve | Review warning | medium | The linear fit reaches the configured R². | Poor linearity can indicate dilution errors, inhibition or a range that is too wide. |
| `std_curve_range` | Standard curve | Review warning | low | The standards with a quantity and a Ct/Cq span at least the configured number of log10 units, lowest to highest quantity. | Slope, efficiency and R² are poorly pinned down by a short curve: the same pipetting scatter moves them more over 2 logs than over 5. The curve was still fitted and reported. |
| `amp_curve_missing` | Amplification | Not assessed | low | Every used well has an amplification curve in the curve file. | Curve checks could not run for these wells. |
| `amp_curve_ambiguous` | Amplification | Not assessed | low | Each curve can be matched to exactly one well and target. | In multiplexed wells a curve without a target label cannot be assigned, so it was not used. |
| `amp_ct_without_rise` | Amplification | Review warning | medium | Wells with a numeric Ct/Cq show a fluorescence rise above baseline noise. | A Ct on a flat curve can be a threshold artefact from noise or drift. |
| `amp_rise_without_ct` | Amplification | Review warning | medium | Wells whose curve rises above baseline noise report a Ct/Cq. | The instrument's threshold or baseline settings may have missed real amplification, or the rise is an artefact. |
| `amp_ct_inconsistent` | Amplification | Review warning | low | The offset between the reported Ct/Cq and the curve's crossing of a common threshold matches the rest of the plate. | A well-specific offset can indicate per-well baseline settings or an unusual curve shape. PlateCheck's threshold is a heuristic. |
| `melt_no_peak` | Melt curve | Review warning | low | Amplified wells show a melt peak of comparable size to the rest of the target. | A Ct without a melt product can indicate a non-specific or artefactual signal. |
| `melt_multiple_peaks` | Melt curve | Review warning | medium | Each well has one main melt peak; secondary peaks at least the configured fraction of the main peak are flagged. | Additional peaks can indicate non-specific products or primer-dimer, which would contribute to the Ct. |
| `melt_tm_deviation` | Melt curve | Review warning | low | The main peak Tm is within the configured tolerance of the target's median Tm. | A shifted Tm can indicate a different product, or a salt or volume difference in that well. |
| `ntc_melt_peak` | Melt curve | Review warning | medium | No-template controls have no melt peak comparable to the target product. | A Tm near the product suggests template contamination; a lower Tm is often primer-dimer. The cause is not certain. |
| `rna_type_reference_mismatch` | RNA type | Review warning | low | In the Analysis step, miRNA targets are not normalised to an mRNA reference gene, and mRNA targets not to a miRNA or snoRNA/snRNA reference. RNA types come from the Genes step, or are guessed from the target name. | A miRNA and an mRNA are usually reverse transcribed in different ways (stem-loop or poly(A) RT against oligo-dT or random primers), so a ΔCt between them also carries the difference between those reactions. References of the same RNA class are preferred. |
| `mirna_no_small_rna_reference` | RNA type | Missing context | low | When the plate has miRNA targets, it also has a small-RNA reference (a snoRNA or snRNA such as U6, RNU44 or RNU48), or a miRNA or spike-in is chosen as reference in the Analysis step. | miRNAs are best normalised to small RNAs that go through the same RT and behave alike in extraction. Without one on the plate, the usual references cannot be applied to these targets. |
| `lncrna_no_nrt` | RNA type | Missing context | low | Each lncRNA target has at least one no-RT well, unless no-RT controls are already expected for every target or marked not applicable. | Many lncRNAs have one or two exons, so primers often cannot span an exon junction, and genomic DNA is then amplified like the RNA. A no-RT control is the way to see whether it was. |
| `missing_metadata` | Method metadata | Missing context | per field (see below) | Key method context (protocol, assay details, efficiency evidence, analysis method, instrument) is recorded. | Missing context is reported as not provided, not as a failed assay. It makes later review and reporting harder. |

## Method metadata fields

| Field | Reported when empty as |
|---|---|
| Protocol name and version | low |
| Assay / primer details (sequences, assay IDs, amplicon) | medium |
| Efficiency / assay validation evidence | medium |
| Analysis method (e.g. ΔΔCq with named reference genes) | low |
| Instrument and software version | low |
| Run settings (cycling, threshold and baseline settings) | info |
| Reagent and primer lot numbers | info |
| Deviations from protocol | not reported |
